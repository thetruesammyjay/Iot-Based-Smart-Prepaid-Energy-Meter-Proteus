## 1.1 Background to the Study

The global demand for electricity continues to grow at an unprecedented rate, driven by population growth, rapid urbanisation, industrialisation, and the proliferation of consumer electronics. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA, 2023), global electricity consumption surpassed 25,000 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2022, a figure projected to double by 2050 as developing economies intensify their electrification programmes. At the core of electricity service delivery lies energy metering, the process by which electrical energy consumption is measured, recorded, and translated into billing obligations between utility providers and consumers. The accuracy, reliability, and accessibility of metering infrastructure are therefore critical determinants of the financial sustainability of electricity distribution networks and the equitable treatment of consumers.

Traditionally, electricity metering has relied on analogue electromechanical meters, devices that measure cumulative energy consumption through the rotation of a metallic disc driven by electromagnetic induction. While these meters have served adequately for decades, they present fundamental limitations that are increasingly incompatible with the demands of a modern, efficient electricity distribution sector. Analogue meters require periodic physical inspection by field agents, a process that is labour-intensive, prone to transcription errors, susceptible to meter manipulation, and operationally costly at scale. In postpaid billing environments, the gap between consumption and payment creates credit risk for utility providers, as consumers may accumulate bills beyond their capacity to repay before disconnection procedures are initiated.

The transition from analogue to digital and subsequently to smart metering represents a paradigm shift in the relationship between electricity consumers and their distribution utilities. Smart metering systems leverage embedded microcontroller technology, digital signal processing, and communication networks to enable real-time measurement of energy parameters (voltage, current, power, and energy) and to support bidirectional communication between the meter and the utility infrastructure. The integration of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies into energy metering extends these capabilities further, enabling remote meter reading, automated billing, demand-side management, and consumer-facing transparency platforms accessible via web and mobile interfaces.

In the Nigerian context, these developments carry particular urgency. Nigeria operates the largest electricity distribution network in Sub-Saharan Africa, yet it faces a structural metering crisis that undermines the financial viability of its Distribution Companies (DisCos) and denies millions of consumers accurate, fair billing. The Nigerian Electricity Supply Industry (NESI) underwent a significant structural transformation between 2005 and 2013, when the Electric Power Sector Reform Act (EPSRA) of 2005 set the legislative foundation for the unbundling and privatisation of the Power Holding Company of Nigeria (PHCN), formerly known as the National Electric Power Authority (NEPA). This privatisation, completed in November 2013, created eleven distribution companies (including Ikeja Electric, Eko Electric, Abuja Electricity Distribution Company (AEDC), Enugu Electricity Distribution Company (EEDC), and others) each operating within defined franchise areas under a regulatory framework administered by the Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC).

Despite this structural reform, a major operational challenge that has persisted across the NESI is the metering gap, defined as the significant disparity between the number of metered and unmetered electricity connections. As of 2022, NERC reported a metering deficit of approximately 7 million connections, meaning that a substantial proportion of active consumers were either unmetered or operating on obsolete, non-functional meters (NERC, 2022). In the absence of functional meters, DisCos have historically resorted to estimated billing, a practice in which monthly charges are assigned to consumers based on assumed or historical consumption averages rather than actual measured usage. This practice has attracted widespread condemnation from consumer advocacy groups and regulatory authorities alike, as it consistently results in overbilling, financial hardship for low-income households, and the erosion of consumer trust in the electricity sector.

The federal government of Nigeria, in recognition of the severity of the metering gap, launched the National Mass Metering Programme (NMMP) in 2020, an initiative designed to accelerate the deployment of functional meters to unmetered consumers across all DisCo franchise areas. Phase 0 of the NMMP targeted the distribution of 1,000,000 meters, funded through the Central Bank of Nigeria's intervention mechanism (Federal Ministry of Power, 2021). While the NMMP represents a significant policy commitment, the pace and technology choices of metering deployment remain subjects of active deliberation. There is a clear technical and economic rationale for deploying smart prepaid meters rather than conventional postpaid digital meters, as the prepaid model eliminates the credit risk inherent in postpaid billing and reduces the administrative burden of invoice generation, debt management, and disconnection enforcement.

Nigeria's Multi-Year Tariff Order (MYTO), maintained by NERC, introduces a further dimension of complexity into the metering environment. The MYTO framework classifies consumers into five service bands (Band A through Band E) based on the daily hours of electricity supply guaranteed by their respective DisCo. Band A consumers, who receive a minimum of twenty hours of supply per day, are subject to significantly higher tariff rates than Band E consumers who receive less than four hours. As of January 2024, NERC published tariff rates ranging from approximately ₦66.00/kWh for Band A residential consumers to ₦40.00/kWh for Band E, with further adjustments expected under the ongoing MYTO review (NERC, 2024). A technically competent smart metering system must therefore incorporate a configurable, band-aware tariff engine capable of reflecting these tiered rate structures and updating them remotely as regulatory changes occur.

The convergence of embedded systems engineering, wireless communication technologies, and cloud computing has given rise to the Internet of Things (IoT) paradigm, a computing architecture in which physical devices embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication modules are networked via internet-based infrastructure to enable autonomous data acquisition, remote actuation, and real-time analytics with minimal direct human intervention. In the electricity distribution sector, IoT-enabled Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) transforms conventional one-way metering into a bidirectional, data-rich communication system. Smart meters equipped with IoT communication interfaces can transmit real-time consumption data to utility management platforms, receive remote configuration commands and tariff updates, execute load control instructions, and dispatch consumer-facing alerts, all without requiring physical field visits. The International Telecommunication Union (ITU, 2022) projects that global IoT device connections will exceed 25 billion by 2030, with smart utility meters constituting one of the most widely deployed application categories. In the specific context of Nigeria, the nationwide penetration of GSM cellular networks operated by MTN Nigeria, Airtel Nigeria, Globacom (Glo), and 9mobile across all thirty-six states and the Federal Capital Territory provides a pre-existing communication infrastructure that IoT-enabled prepaid meters can leverage for consumer interaction and real-time data reporting to utility platforms, without necessitating the capital expenditure associated with building proprietary communication networks. The prepaid metering model, in particular, aligns well with the mobile-money and airtime top-up culture already prevalent among Nigerian consumers, making the transition to token-based electricity recharge operationally intuitive and socially acceptable at the household level.

The proliferation of low-cost, high-integration microcontroller platforms has fundamentally lowered the barrier to entry for intelligent embedded electronic system development, rendering it technically and financially feasible for academic researchers, engineering students, and small-scale manufacturers in developing economies to prototype sophisticated electronic devices without the capital investment historically associated with proprietary embedded development environments or application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) development. The Espressif ESP32 system-on-chip (SoC) represents a particularly compelling platform for IoT-oriented embedded applications such as smart energy metering. The ESP32 integrates a dual-core Xtensa LX6 32-bit processor operating at up to 240 MHz with 4 megabytes of SPI flash memory and 520 kilobytes of static random-access memory (SRAM). Critically, the ESP32 incorporates fully integrated 802.11 b/g/n Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Classic/BLE communication transceivers on a single chip, eliminating the need for external wireless communication modules and substantially reducing circuit complexity and bill-of-materials cost in IoT designs. Its 12-bit successive-approximation analogue-to-digital converter (ADC) with eighteen multiplexed input channels, hardware-based I2C bus controller, multiple full-duplex Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter (UART) channels, and a Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI) bus provide a comprehensive peripheral set capable of supporting the complete interfacing requirements of an advanced smart energy metering system. The ESP32 is fully supported by the Arduino development framework via the official Espressif Arduino Core, enabling access to the same broad ecosystem of community-maintained open-source libraries that have made the Arduino framework a dominant choice for embedded systems prototyping in academic and research contexts worldwide. The Arduino IDE, paired with the ESP32 Arduino Core, provides tested and well-documented software abstractions covering TFT display drivers, UART-based energy module interfaces, Wi-Fi networking stacks, and non-volatile storage libraries, significantly accelerating firmware development relative to bare-metal register-level programming.

Simulation-driven development, facilitated by electronic design automation (EDA) tools such as Proteus Design Suite 8, represents an established and rigorous methodology for the design, validation, and documentation of embedded electronic systems. Proteus enables the construction of complete virtual circuit environments in which microcontroller components (loaded with compiled firmware HEX binaries generated by the Arduino IDE) interact in real time with virtual representations of analogue and digital peripherals, signal sources, measuring instruments, and communication terminals. All electrical signal interactions, I2C bus transactions, UART communication sequences, ADC sample values, and relay switching events observable on the Proteus schematic reflect the actual execution of the embedded firmware, providing a faithful and reproducible virtual analogue of physical hardware behaviour. For an embedded IoT project of the scope described in this study, encompassing analogue sensing circuits, multi-device I2C bus communication, UART channels, relay switching logic, NVS persistence, and cloud-oriented IoT data flow, the simulation environment serves several critical engineering functions: it enables systematic detection and correction of circuit design errors before hardware procurement; it provides a risk-free and repeatable platform for validating firmware logic, including interrupt-driven routines, state machine transitions, and peripheral initialisation sequences; it generates documented simulation evidence (oscilloscope waveforms, virtual terminal logs, TFT LCD screenshots) appropriate for inclusion in academic project reports and technical presentations; and it significantly reduces the total project cost and timeline by ensuring that designs entering physical prototyping are functionally validated. The adoption of Proteus as the primary validation environment in this study is therefore both methodologically rigorous and practically appropriate for an undergraduate engineering project conducted within the resource and infrastructure constraints characteristic of Nigerian academic institutions.

Against this backdrop, the present study proposes the design and implementation of an Advanced IoT-Based Smart Prepaid Energy Meter, simulated using Proteus Design Suite 8. The system integrates an ESP32 microcontroller, a PZEM-004T dedicated energy measurement module, a 1.8-inch TFT LCD display, a relay-based load control mechanism, a push button interface, and the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module for cloud-based data publication to an IoT monitoring dashboard. A configurable prepaid billing engine implements token-based credit recharge mechanisms modelled on the Standard Transfer Specification (STS) IEC 62055-41 standard. The Proteus simulation environment provides a rigorous virtual testing platform for validating all hardware and firmware interactions before any physical prototyping, in alignment with best practices in software and embedded systems engineering.

## 1.2 Statement of Problem

The operational and financial inefficiencies inherent in Nigeria's conventional postpaid electricity metering infrastructure constitute the central problem that this study seeks to address. Despite ongoing reform efforts within the Nigerian Electricity Supply Industry (NESI), the following specific challenges continue to persist and collectively degrade the quality of electricity service delivery:

i. **Widespread Estimated Billing:** The metering gap in Nigeria has created a systemic dependence on estimated billing by Distribution Companies (DisCos). Unmetered consumers are assigned monthly electricity charges based on assumed consumption rather than actual measurement, resulting in arbitrary and frequently excessive invoices that impose disproportionate financial burdens on residential and small business consumers. This practice undermines the principle of accurate, evidence-based billing and generates persistent consumer-utility disputes.

ii. **Absence of Real-Time Consumption Visibility:** Conventional analogue and basic digital meters provide consumers with no mechanism to monitor their energy consumption in real time. This informational asymmetry prevents proactive energy conservation, makes it impossible for consumers to verify the accuracy of their bills, and limits their ability to make informed decisions regarding appliance usage and spending on electricity.

iii. **Revenue Leakage and Non-Technical Losses:** The postpaid billing model creates an inherent credit risk for DisCos, as consumers are billed retroactively for energy already consumed. Delayed payments, debt accumulation, and fraudulent meter bypass practices collectively contribute to non-technical losses that NERC has estimated at 38 to 45 percent of distributed energy across several franchise areas (NERC, 2022). These losses directly reduce the financial viability of DisCos and reduce their capacity for infrastructure investment and maintenance.

iv. **Operational Inefficiency of Manual Meter Reading:** Physical meter reading operations require large teams of field agents deployed periodically to consumer premises. This process is expensive, time-consuming, susceptible to human error, and inaccessible in locations where safety or access constraints prevent field visits. The associated administrative overhead diverts DisCo resources from core distribution infrastructure improvement.

v. **Disconnection and Reconnection Complexity:** In cases where consumers default on payment obligations, disconnection and subsequent reconnection require physical intervention by field technicians, incurring both direct operational costs and service interruption delays. The absence of a remote load control capability prevents DisCos from implementing efficient demand management and credit enforcement policies.

vi. **Absence of IoT-Enabled Remote Utility Management:** Existing metering infrastructure in most Nigerian DisCo franchise areas lacks the communication architecture required to support a centralised, data-driven utility management platform. Without real-time consumption data aggregated across thousands of meters, DisCos are unable to perform accurate demand forecasting, outage detection, or targeted tariff analysis.

vii. **Meter Tampering and Energy Theft:** The absence of functional metering across a substantial proportion of Nigerian electricity connections, combined with limited enforcement infrastructure and the absence of real-time monitoring capability in conventional metering systems, has created conditions in which energy theft through meter bypass, illegal service connections, and physical meter tampering has become a significant contributor to commercial losses across the NESI. NERC's performance review assessments have identified meter-related losses as a material component of the aggregate technical and commercial losses recorded by DisCos, with several franchise areas reporting total combined losses exceeding fifty percent of distributed energy across their networks (NERC, 2022). Conventional electromechanical meters provide no electronic tamper-detection capability and no mechanism for remote disconnection upon identification of anomalous consumption patterns, rendering them structurally inadequate as instruments for loss reduction in high-theft-risk environments. A smart prepaid metering architecture, incorporating relay-based remote load control, NVS-based event logging, and IoT-based anomaly reporting to a centralised utility management platform, provides a substantially stronger structural deterrent against energy theft and a more responsive detection and response capability than is achievable with any conventional analogue or basic digital metering solution.

These problems, collectively, represent a systemic failure of the conventional metering paradigm to meet the requirements of a modern, commercially sustainable electricity distribution sector. The design and implementation of an IoT-based smart prepaid energy meter, as proposed in this study, directly addresses each of these identified deficiencies through the integration of real-time measurement, automated billing, remote load control, and cloud-based data management.

## 1.3 Objectives of the Study

**General Objective**

The general objective of this study is to design and implement an Advanced IoT-Based Smart Prepaid Energy Meter (simulated in Proteus Design Suite 8) that accurately measures electrical energy consumption in real time, enforces a prepaid billing model, and leverages IoT communication infrastructure to enable remote consumer interaction and utility-side data management within the Nigerian electricity distribution context.

**Specific Objectives**

In furtherance of the general objective, the following specific objectives are pursued:

1. To design a microcontroller-based circuit, using the ESP32, capable of measuring real-time AC voltage, current, active power, and cumulative energy consumption in kilowatt-hours (kWh) using the PZEM-004T dedicated energy measurement module.

2. To implement a configurable prepaid billing engine that deducts credit, denominated in Nigerian Naira (₦), from a stored balance in proportion to instantaneous energy consumption, and that supports NERC Multi-Year Tariff Order (MYTO) Band A–E rate structures.

3. To develop and integrate a relay-based automatic load disconnection and reconnection mechanism that engages when the credit balance reaches zero and disengages upon successful credit recharge.

4. To implement a token-based recharge mechanism modelled on the principles of the Standard Transfer Specification (STS) IEC 62055-41, enabling secure, one-time-use numeric credit tokens to be entered via the push button interface or delivered remotely via an IoT dashboard.

5. To leverage the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module to publish real-time energy consumption data and meter status to an IoT cloud platform (ThingSpeak), and to receive remote recharge commands from a utility operator dashboard.

6. To ensure persistent storage of meter state - including credit balance, cumulative energy consumption, relay state, and used token records - in the ESP32 Non-Volatile Storage (NVS) subsystem, guaranteeing data integrity across power cycles.

7. To simulate the complete hardware and firmware system within Proteus Design Suite 8, producing a fully functional virtual prototype that validates all subsystem interactions prior to physical implementation.

## 1.4 Research Questions

In pursuit of the stated objectives, this study is guided by the following research questions:

1. How can a microcontroller-based embedded system accurately measure real-time AC voltage, current, active power, and cumulative energy in kilowatt-hours using low-cost, commercially available sensing components?

2. What billing algorithm design and EEPROM persistence strategy best ensures accurate, tamper-resistant credit deduction and reliable state recovery following unexpected power interruptions in a prepaid energy metering system?

3. How can Standard Transfer Specification (STS) token principles be effectively adapted and implemented within the resource constraints of an ESP32 microcontroller for use in a simulated prepaid metering environment?

4. To what extent can Proteus Design Suite 8 serve as a comprehensive virtual validation platform for an IoT-integrated embedded system encompassing microcontroller firmware, analogue sensing circuits, I2C peripheral communication, relay control, and UART-based communication module interactions?

5. How can IoT communication technologies - specifically the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module - be integrated into an embedded metering system to support real-time consumer alerts, remote recharge operations, and centralised energy data publication in the context of Nigerian telecommunications infrastructure?

## 1.5 Scope of the Study

This study is scoped to the design, firmware development, and Proteus-based simulation of a single-phase Advanced IoT-Based Smart Prepaid Energy Meter. The scope is defined across the following dimensions:

**Technical Scope:** The study encompasses the end-to-end design of the complete hardware circuit schematic within Proteus Design Suite 8, the development of modular, layered embedded C++ firmware for the ESP32 microcontroller using the Arduino framework and IDE, and the comprehensive functional simulation of all hardware and firmware subsystems within the Proteus virtual environment. The hardware subsystems within scope include: real-time AC energy measurement using a PZEM-004T dedicated energy measurement module communicating with the ESP32 via UART using the Modbus RTU protocol; a configurable prepaid billing engine with tariff parameters and balance state persisted in the ESP32 Non-Volatile Storage (NVS) subsystem using the Preferences library; automated relay-based load disconnection triggered by credit balance conditions; a 1.8-inch TFT LCD display interface for real-time consumer-facing data rendering via SPI; a push button interface for user interaction and menu navigation; and the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module for IoT platform data publishing, validated in simulation via a Proteus Virtual Terminal. The firmware architecture is organised into a Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL), a Service Layer, a Business Logic Layer, and an Application Layer, in conformance with standard layered embedded software design conventions. The system is designed for single-phase alternating current (AC) loads operating at the standard Nigerian electricity supply voltage of 230V root mean square (RMS) (±10%) at a mains frequency of 50Hz, consistent with the technical standards applicable to residential and light commercial electricity consumers across all Nigerian DisCo franchise areas as defined under the NERC Distribution Code.

**Geographic and Market Scope:** The billing engine, tariff configuration, consumer alert messages, and phone number format are specifically designed for deployment within the Nigerian electricity market, with reference to NERC's MYTO Band A–E tariff structure and the telecommunications numbering plan of the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC). All monetary values are denominated in Nigerian Naira (₦).

**Simulation Scope:** The physical Radio Frequency (RF) communication capabilities of the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module are outside the simulation scope of Proteus. The Wi-Fi communication interface is represented in the simulation by a Virtual Terminal component that displays network communication data sequences, enabling validation of the firmware communication logic without live network connectivity.

**Exclusions:** The study does not include three-phase metering, full cryptographic implementation of the STS DKGA04 token generation algorithm (a simplified lookup-based model is used), physical PCB fabrication, enclosure design, or field deployment. Tamper-detection hardware and anti-reverse engineering measures, while identified as important for physical deployment, are outside the scope of this simulation study.

## 1.6 Limitations of the Study

The conduct of this study is subject to the following constraints and limitations, which are acknowledged in the interest of academic transparency:

i. **Simulation Environment Constraints:** The Proteus Design Suite, while a highly capable virtual prototyping platform, does not replicate all physical phenomena present in real hardware. The absence of ADC quantisation noise, thermal drift in analogue components, electromagnetic interference, and supply voltage variation means that the performance of the firmware algorithms may appear more ideal in simulation than would be observed in a physical prototype operating under field conditions.

ii. **RF Communication Simulation:** The ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module cannot be functionally simulated in Proteus, as the software does not support RF signal modelling or TCP/IP network stack emulation. The Wi-Fi communication logic is validated through a Virtual Terminal component, but end-to-end live IoT platform publishing and over-the-air token delivery cannot be demonstrated within the Proteus environment. Full validation of the Wi-Fi subsystem requires physical hardware deployment.

iii. **STS Token Cryptography Simplification:** The Standard Transfer Specification (STS) IEC 62055-41 mandates the use of the DKGA04 key derivation and encryption algorithm for generating and validating recharge tokens. Full implementation of this algorithm is beyond the computational verification scope of a Proteus simulation and requires a dedicated secure server-side token management system. Accordingly, this study implements a simplified token model using a pre-defined lookup table stored in program memory (PROGMEM), which validates the structural and logical behaviour of the token system without replicating its cryptographic security properties.

iv. **Single-Phase Operation Only:** The design addresses single-phase residential and light commercial loads, which represent the majority of consumer connections addressed by the NMMP. Industrial and large commercial consumers operating on three-phase supply are outside the scope of this study.

v. **Resource Constraints of the ESP32:** The ESP32 microcontroller provides 4MB of SPI flash memory and 520KB of SRAM. While these resources are substantially larger than conventional 8-bit microcontroller platforms, the concurrent execution of the Wi-Fi networking stack, TFT display rendering pipeline, UART-based PZEM-004T communication, and billing computation within a single cooperative firmware loop may approach the practical runtime memory limits of the available SRAM under peak load conditions.

vi. **Access to Current Regulatory Data:** Tariff rates referenced in this study are drawn from NERC's publicly available MYTO documentation as of January 2024. These rates are subject to revision by the regulatory commission, and the figures cited may not reflect rates applicable at the time of physical deployment.

vii. **Hardware Procurement and Laboratory Infrastructure Constraints:** The physical validation of an embedded IoT system of this scope, encompassing analogue AC sensing circuits, UART-based communication modules, cloud-connected data pipelines, and real-time relay switching, requires access to laboratory instrumentation including digital oscilloscopes, regulated DC power supplies, function generators, and a live Wi-Fi internet connection for IoT platform testing. Such resources may not be consistently available within the laboratory infrastructure of Nigerian academic institutions, particularly in the context of power supply intermittency and equipment maintenance challenges. This constraint further reinforces the appropriateness and necessity of Proteus simulation as the primary validation methodology for this project. The absence of physical hardware testing means that certain performance characteristics, including long-term NVS write endurance behaviour, component thermal drift under sustained operating conditions, and real-world current measurement accuracy under reactive or non-linear load conditions, cannot be empirically characterised within the scope of this study and are deferred to future physical prototyping phases.

## 1.7 Significance of the Study

The outcomes of this study carry significance across multiple dimensions, encompassing technical innovation, socioeconomic impact, regulatory alignment, and academic contribution:

**Contribution to the Nigerian Electricity Sector:** This study directly contributes to the objectives of the National Mass Metering Programme (NMMP) by demonstrating a technically feasible, low-cost smart prepaid metering design that can serve as a reference model for local meter manufacturers, technology start-ups, and DisCos engaged in metering expansion programmes under the NMMP framework. The use of commercially available, low-cost components, centred on the ESP32 platform, means that the bill of materials cost for a prototype unit is significantly lower than that of commercially procured smart meters, potentially enabling local fabrication and reducing the foreign exchange burden associated with mass meter importation. By eliminating estimated billing, automating credit enforcement, enabling remote load disconnection, and providing a real-time IoT data reporting pipeline, the proposed system directly addresses the root causes of revenue leakage and consumer grievances that have consistently undermined the commercial performance and investment attractiveness of Nigeria's electricity distribution sector since privatisation.

**Benefit to Electricity Consumers:** For the millions of Nigerian consumers currently subject to estimated billing, the deployment of smart prepaid metering technology represents a direct, measurable improvement in service quality, billing fairness, and financial autonomy. Consumers gain real-time visibility into their energy consumption and remaining credit balance through the on-device TFT LCD display and, remotely, through the IoT dashboard, eliminating the financial uncertainty and anxiety caused by arbitrary, unpredictable estimated invoices. The token-based recharge model supports flexible, incremental credit loading in denominations as small as the consumer chooses, which is particularly well-suited to the income patterns of Nigeria's low-income and informal-sector households, who often manage finances on a daily or weekly basis rather than on a monthly billing cycle. This model parallels the pay-as-you-go mobile telecommunications paradigm that has already demonstrated broad consumer acceptance across Nigeria and Sub-Saharan Africa more broadly.

**Support for NERC Regulatory Objectives:** The system's configurable MYTO Band A–E tariff engine, real-time consumption measurement capability, and IoT-based data reporting architecture collectively support NERC's core regulatory mandate for accurate metering, evidence-based billing, consumer protection, and service quality monitoring. The granular, timestamped consumption and balance data published to the IoT platform can serve as the evidentiary basis for regulatory compliance reporting by DisCos, demand-side management programme design, service band performance auditing, and informed tariff review proceedings. Furthermore, the relay-based automatic load control mechanism enables DisCos to enforce credit conditions and manage peak demand load programmatically, supporting grid stability goals within franchise areas experiencing constrained electricity allocation from transmission companies.

**Academic and Technical Contribution:** This study contributes to the body of knowledge in the intersecting fields of IoT systems engineering, embedded software design, and electricity distribution technology by demonstrating the principled application of software engineering design patterns, including layered architecture, modular decomposition, hardware abstraction, the single-responsibility principle, and non-blocking cooperative scheduling via `millis()`-based time management, applied to a real-world embedded IoT energy metering application. The Proteus simulation methodology employed provides a fully documented, replicable virtual validation framework that can serve as a methodological template for subsequent researchers and students undertaking embedded systems or IoT hardware-software co-design projects within Nigerian and African academic institutions, particularly in environments where consistent access to laboratory hardware is constrained.

**Contribution to the Discipline of Software Engineering:** By rigorously applying formal software engineering practices, including requirements specification, layered and modular firmware architecture, module-level interface documentation through header files, version-controlled source code management, firmware configuration management through preprocessor constants, and structured black-box test case specification with defined inputs, expected outputs, and validation methods, to an embedded systems project in the critical infrastructure domain of electricity metering, this study demonstrates the depth, versatility, and practical societal impact of the Software Engineering discipline. It illustrates that the analytical and design tools of software engineering are not confined to enterprise information systems or web application development, but are equally applicable to, and equally necessary in, the design of safety-critical, resource-constrained embedded systems that directly affect the quality of life of millions of people.

## 1.8 Definition of Terms

The following terms are defined as used within the context of this study:

**PZEM-004T:** A dedicated alternating current energy measurement module manufactured by PEACEFAIR. It provides direct digital measurement of line voltage (80-260V), load current (0-100A), active power (0-23kW), cumulative energy (0-9999.99 kWh), mains frequency (45-65Hz), and power factor (0.00-1.00) via a UART serial interface using the Modbus RTU communication protocol. Unlike ADC-based sensing approaches, the PZEM-004T performs internal calibration and signal conditioning, eliminating the need for external voltage dividers, separate current sensors, and firmware-level RMS computation algorithms. In this study, the PZEM-004T module serves as the sole energy measurement front-end, providing pre-computed electrical parameter values to the ESP32 microcontroller via UART.

**Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI):** A collective term for the systems and networks (including smart meters, communication modules, data management software, and back-office platforms) that enable two-way communication between electricity consumers and their distribution utility.

**Alternating Current (AC):** An electrical current whose magnitude and direction reverse periodically, typically following a sinusoidal waveform. In Nigeria, the standard residential and commercial electricity supply is single-phase AC at a nominal voltage of 230V RMS and a frequency of 50Hz. The smart meter in this study is designed to measure and bill AC energy consumption.

**Analogue-to-Digital Converter (ADC):** A hardware component that converts a continuous analogue voltage signal into a discrete digital numerical value representable in binary form. The ESP32 integrates a 12-bit ADC capable of resolving input voltages between 0V and 3.3V into 4,096 discrete integer steps. In this study, the ADC is not used for primary energy measurement, as the PZEM-004T module handles all AC signal measurement and provides pre-computed digital parameter values to the ESP32 via UART.

**Embedded System:** A dedicated computing system designed to perform one or more specific functions within a larger mechanical or electronic system, characterised by tight resource constraints (processor speed, memory, power consumption) and real-time operational requirements. The smart meter designed in this study is an embedded system, with the ESP32 microcontroller serving as its processing core.

**Firmware:** Software code that is permanently or semi-permanently programmed into the read-only or flash memory of an embedded device and controls the low-level operation of its hardware. Unlike application software running on general-purpose operating systems, firmware executes directly on hardware with no operating system abstraction layer. In this study, the firmware is written in embedded C++ using the Arduino framework and compiled to a HEX binary loaded into the ESP32 microcontroller.

**I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit):** A two-wire serial communication bus protocol developed by Philips Semiconductor (now NXP), operating on a Serial Data Line (SDA) and a Serial Clock Line (SCL). I2C supports multiple devices on a shared bus, each identified by a unique 7-bit address. In this study, the ESP32's I2C interface is available for peripheral expansion; however, the primary peripherals in this design - the PZEM-004T energy module and the TFT LCD display - communicate via UART and SPI respectively.

**Root Mean Square (RMS):** A statistical measure of the magnitude of a varying quantity, calculated as the square root of the arithmetic mean of the squares of instantaneous values sampled over a complete cycle. For AC voltage and current waveforms, the RMS value is the equivalent DC magnitude that delivers the same power to a resistive load. In this study, V_RMS and I_RMS are computed in firmware from ADC samples to determine active power consumption.

**UART (Universal Asynchronous Receiver-Transmitter):** A hardware component that facilitates asynchronous serial communication between devices using two signal lines - a transmit line (TX) and a receive line (RX). Communication parameters including baud rate, data bits, stop bits, and parity must be configured identically on both communicating devices. In this study, the ESP32's hardware UART is used to communicate with the PZEM-004T energy measurement module via the Modbus RTU protocol. The ESP32 provides multiple hardware UART channels, eliminating the need for software-emulated serial communication.

**ESP32:** A low-power, dual-core 32-bit Xtensa LX6 microcontroller system-on-chip (SoC) developed by Espressif Systems. It integrates a fully functional 802.11 b/g/n Wi-Fi transceiver and Bluetooth Classic/BLE module on-chip, alongside 4MB of SPI flash memory and 520KB of SRAM. The ESP32 supports the Arduino development framework via the Espressif Arduino Core and serves as the primary computational, communication, and control unit of the smart meter design in this study.

**Arduino Framework:** An open-source programming framework and integrated development environment (IDE) that provides a simplified C++ programming abstraction for embedded microcontroller development. Originally developed for Atmel AVR microcontrollers, the Arduino framework has been extended to support a wide range of microcontroller platforms including the Espressif ESP32. In this study, the ESP32 firmware is developed using the Arduino framework via the Espressif Arduino Core, enabling access to standard library functions and a broad ecosystem of community-maintained third-party libraries.

**Distribution Company (DisCo):** Any of the eleven electricity distribution companies created by the privatisation of PHCN in 2013, each licensed by NERC to distribute and retail electricity within a defined geographic franchise area in Nigeria.

**EEPROM (Electrically Erasable Programmable Read-Only Memory):** A type of non-volatile memory that retains stored data in the absence of power supply. In this study, EEPROM is used to persistently store the meter's credit balance, cumulative energy consumption, relay state, and used token records.

**Estimated Billing:** The practice of charging electricity consumers for assumed energy consumption in the absence of a functional meter, typically based on historical usage averages or flat-rate assessments. Estimated billing is associated with widespread consumer complaints of overbilling in the Nigerian electricity sector.

**SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface):** A synchronous serial communication protocol using four signal lines: MOSI (Master Out Slave In), MISO (Master In Slave Out), SCK (Serial Clock), and CS (Chip Select). SPI enables high-speed full-duplex data transfer between a master device and one or more slave peripherals. In this study, the SPI bus is used to communicate between the ESP32 and the 1.8-inch TFT LCD display module.

**Internet of Things (IoT):** A paradigm in which physical devices (embedded with sensors, actuators, and communication hardware) are interconnected via the internet to collect, exchange, and act upon data with minimal human intervention.

**Metering Gap:** The quantitative shortfall between the number of active electricity connections requiring functional meters and the number of such connections that are actually metered. Nigeria's metering gap was estimated at approximately 7 million connections as of 2022 (NERC, 2022).

**MYTO (Multi-Year Tariff Order):** The regulatory instrument issued by NERC establishing the tariff framework for electricity supply in Nigeria. The MYTO defines cost-reflective electricity tariff rates across service bands (A–E) based on guaranteed hours of daily supply and consumer category.

**National Mass Metering Programme (NMMP):** A federal government initiative launched in 2020 to accelerate the deployment of functional electricity meters to unmetered consumers across all DisCo franchise areas in Nigeria, partially funded through a Central Bank of Nigeria intervention loan facility.

**NERC (Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission):** The independent government agency established by the Electric Power Sector Reform Act (EPSRA) of 2005, responsible for licensing, tariff regulation, consumer protection, and standards enforcement within the Nigerian Electricity Supply Industry (NESI).

**Proteus Design Suite:** A proprietary electronic design automation (EDA) software package developed by Labcenter Electronics Ltd., supporting schematic capture, circuit simulation (SPICE-based), mixed-signal simulation, microcontroller firmware co-simulation, and PCB layout design. Proteus 8 is the primary design and simulation tool used in this study.

**Relay:** An electromechanical or solid-state switching device that opens or closes an electrical circuit in response to a control signal. In this study, a 5V single-channel electromechanical relay is used to connect or disconnect the consumer load based on the credit balance status.

**Real-Time Clock (RTC):** An integrated circuit that maintains accurate timekeeping independently of the main microcontroller, typically powered by a dedicated backup battery. In this study, the ESP32 microcontroller's internal real-time clock, synchronised via the Network Time Protocol (NTP) over Wi-Fi, provides date and time data for consumption log timestamping.

**Smart Meter:** An electronic electricity meter that records energy consumption at configurable intervals, supports real-time or near-real-time two-way communication with a utility management system, and may incorporate prepaid billing, demand response, and remote load control capabilities.

**Standard Transfer Specification (STS):** An open international standard (IEC 62055-41) defining the protocol for the transfer of credit and other information from an electricity utility to a consumer's prepaid meter using encrypted numeric tokens. The STS standard underpins the token-based recharge mechanism implemented in this study.

**ThingSpeak:** A cloud-based IoT analytics platform developed by MathWorks that provides RESTful API endpoints for data ingestion, storage, visualisation, and alerting. In this study, ThingSpeak serves as the IoT cloud backend to which the smart meter publishes real-time energy readings via the ESP32 built-in Wi-Fi module.

**TFT LCD (Thin-Film Transistor Liquid Crystal Display):** A type of active-matrix liquid crystal display in which each pixel is controlled by a dedicated thin-film transistor, enabling higher contrast, faster refresh rates, and richer colour reproduction than passive-matrix or character-segment displays. The 1.8-inch TFT LCD module used in this study (based on the ST7735 or ILI9163 display driver) provides a 128 x 160 pixel colour display interface via SPI, enabling graphical rendering of real-time energy parameters, credit balance, and system status information for the consumer.
